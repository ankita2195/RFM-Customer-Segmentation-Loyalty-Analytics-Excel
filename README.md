# RFM Customer Segmentation and Retention Analytics using Excel

## Business Problem
How can a business identify its most valuable customers, recognise early signs of disengagement, and turn customer purchasing behaviour into targeted strategies that drive loyalty, repeat purchases, and revenue retention?

- Identify high-value, regular and inactive customers.
- Understand customer purchasing patterns and revenue contribution.
- Identify customers at risk of disengagement.
- Develop targeted loyalty programs and retention strategies for different customer segments.

---

## Project Overview
Using RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis, segmented customers based on their purchasing behaviour and develop targeted loyalty and retention strategies.

Built entirely in Microsoft Excel, the project combines customer-level RFM scoring, interactive dashboard reporting, tier-based loyalty planning, and targeted communication strategies to translate customer data into actionable business recommendations.

---

## Dataset Overview
- 100 Customers
- 341 Transactions
- ₹8,80,130 Total Revenue
- 7 Cities

The workbook retains the original transaction data for reference and traceability.

---

## Tools & Techniques

- Microsoft Excel
- RFM Analysis and Quintile-based Scoring
- Pivot Tables and Pivot Charts
- Customer Segmentation

---

## Methodology
Customers were evaluated using three dimensions:

- **Recency** (R): Days since the customer's last purchase. Lower recency indicates more recent activity.

- **Frequency** (F): Total number of purchases made by the customer.

- **Monetary** (M): Total amount spent by the customer.

Each dimension was assigned a score from 1 to 5 using customer quintiles. Recency scoring was reversed because more recent purchases indicate stronger engagement.

| Tier | RFM Score | Customer Profile |
|---|---|---|
| Elite | 12–15 | Recent, frequent and high-value customers |
| Growth | 8–11 | Moderately active customers with growth potential |
| Re-Engage | 3–7 | Infrequent, low-value or inactive customers |

---

## Key Findings
- Elite customers represent 28% of the customer base but contribute 48.4% of total revenue, making retention crucial.
- Growth customers account for 31% of customers and contribute 33.6% of revenue, representing an opportunity for increased engagement and repeat purchases.
- Re-Engage customers form 41% of the customer base but contribute only 18% of revenue.
- 40 customers are classified as dormant based on their Recency Score.
- 39 customers fall under the Win-Back opportunity category, while 6 Elite customers are classified as Elite At Risk.

## Loyalty & Retention Strategy
A three-tier loyalty program was developed based on customer segments:

- **Elite**: Focus on retention through VIP benefits, early access and personalized recommendations. Win-back Elite at risk.

- **Growth**: Encourage repeat purchases and migration towards the Elite tier through bundles and targeted incentives.

- **Re-Engage**: Reactivate dormant customers through personalised win-back campaigns and limited-time offers.

## Conclusion

The analysis highlights a clear difference between customer distribution and revenue contribution. While Elite customers make up just 28% of the customer base, they generate 48.4% of total revenue. Whereas, Re-Engage customers account for 41% of customers but contribute only 18% of revenue.

The segmentation provides a structured approach to prioritising customer relationships, strengthening loyalty among high-value customers, encouraging repeat purchases, and reactivating disengaged customers.

By combining RFM analysis with targeted loyalty and communication strategies, the project demonstrates how customer data can support more focused retention decisions and sustainable revenue growth.
